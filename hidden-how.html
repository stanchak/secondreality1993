HOW THE HIDDEN PART WORKS

A DENSE SKY OUT OF 512 STARS

512 stars are projected into a 320×100 bitmap and ORed into a ring of 64 buffers — half a megabyte of EMS, one page at a time. Nothing clears them, so each buffer keeps every 64th frame it is handed: a few hundred stars become a dense sky, and cycling the ring makes it shimmer. With no decay it thickens for as long as you watch. The screen shows the current buffer up top and the buffer from 32 frames ago, mirrored, below — the bottom of the screen is the top of the starfield, half a second late.

TWO IMAGES INTERLEAVED

The mode is 320×400 in 16 colours — mode 0Dh with CRTC 9’s scan doubling switched off — and every blit steps two rows at a time. Stars take the 200 even rows, the text the odd ones. Interleaving rather than overlapping is why the star blits’ text-band clipping is commented out in the shipped build and the text survives anyway — and it is where the text gets its translucency: a black bar with starfield showing through every other line.

The loader starts MUSIC0 at order 70 for it — eight patterns fenced off by 255 end-markers, reached from nowhere else. Past that there is no music sync at all: no dis_sync, no musplus. The timeline is a frame counter at 70 Hz, ending when you stop watching, because dis_exit was a keypress.
